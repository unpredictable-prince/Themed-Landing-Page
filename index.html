Three decisions that shaped the design

Intent, not twitch

Surface electrodes read muscle contraction, which is a late and noisy signal. The cuff sits against the nerve bundle and picks up the instruction on its way out. You get the grip you meant, not the one your forearm managed.

It learns your hands

The first fortnight is mostly the arm watching you. Grips you use daily move to the front of the pattern queue; ones you never call get retired. A guitarist and a joiner end up with visibly different arms.

It fails quietly

Lose power mid-grip and the hand holds position rather than dropping what it has. A recessed lever under the wrist releases it by hand. No alarm, no flashing, nothing that turns a flat battery into an event in a supermarket queue.

How fitting works

Consultation

A clinician checks residual limb length, skin condition and what you actually need the arm to do. Some people leave here with a different recommendation, and that is a fine outcome.

One appointment

Socket cast

A silicone impression and a pressure map. The socket is the part that decides whether you wear the arm all day or take it off at two in the afternoon, so it gets the most attention.

Two weeks to first fit

Nerve mapping

You imagine movements while the cuff records. It takes about an hour and feels like nothing at all. The map it produces is yours and does not transfer between arms.

90 minutes

Fitting

Socket adjusted on site, wrist alignment set to your shoulder, grips loaded. You go home with it the same day.

Half a day

Calibration and training

Six sessions over three months, then annually. Most of it is unlearning the compensations you built up before the arm arrived.

Three months, tapering
